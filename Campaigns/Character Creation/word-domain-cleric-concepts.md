# THREE WAYS TO PLAY A WORD DOMAIN CLERIC

## Character concepts for the homebrew Cleric subclass

*Built against the 2024 Player's Handbook rules in this repository, the Word Domain in `Homebrew/homebrew-cleric-word-domain.md`, and the homebrew backgrounds in `Homebrew/homebrew-backgrounds.md`. As with `bravo-fighter-concepts.md`, each concept is sited in one of the three regions of the shared setting, but all three port cleanly to any table.*

---

## Part 0: What the Word Domain Actually Rewards

The Word Domain is the most **Wisdom-pure** subclass in the book and the most **action-hungry**. Every feature either costs your Magic action, costs your Channel Divinity, or costs your Concentration — sometimes two of the three. The design work is not in choosing a weapon; it is in deciding what you are willing to spend a turn on.

**1. Wisdom is the only ability that scales this subclass — including Havoc.** This surprises people. Havoc reads "must succeed on a Charisma saving throw against your spell save DC," which looks like a Charisma feature. It isn't: *the target* rolls Charisma, and the DC is 8 + your Wisdom modifier + your Proficiency Bonus. Your own Charisma contributes nothing to it. All three builds below run Charisma between 8 and 13 and lose nothing that matters.

**2. Divine Orator is worth about +3.5 to Persuasion — roughly seven points of Charisma.** Advantage on a d20 is worth about +3.3 to +3.7 across normal DCs. A Word cleric with Charisma 10 and proficiency rolls spoken Persuasion at an effective +5.5 at level 3 and +8.5 by level 13, which is party-face territory without spending a single point on the stat. Two caveats: the Advantage applies only to checks *that involve speaking* (nothing written, nothing done by gesture or presence), and only to Persuasion — Deception and Intimidation get nothing, and neither is on the Cleric skill list anyway. **Persuasion is on the Cleric list. Take it.**

**3. Divine Orator's second clause is the real prize, and it pairs with one specific spell.** *Silence* blocks Verbal components inside a 20-foot Sphere. Divine Orator exempts you from that. Inside your own *Silence* you are the only creature in the room who can cast a spell with a Verbal component — which is very nearly every spell an enemy caster has prepared. Three catches, all of which matter:

  - *Silence* costs your **Concentration**, so on Silence turns you are not also running *Spirit Guardians*.
  - Everyone inside the Sphere is **Deafened**, which switches off your own *Suggestion* (it requires a target "that can hear and understand you") and makes *Command* a table question — see the DM questions below.
  - *Silence* blocks Verbal only. Somatic-only spells still work, and a handful of monsters cast without components at all.

**4. War Caster is the other half of Divine Orator.** Divine Orator makes your voice unstoppable; War Caster makes your hands unnecessary — you can perform Somatic components with a shield and a holy symbol occupying both hands. Together they mean a Word cleric can cast, gagged, grappled, silenced, shield up, in any condition short of Incapacitated. Add Advantage on Concentration saves and it stops being a nice feat and starts being load-bearing. Every build below takes it, most of them at level 8.

**5. The domain spell table is worth about ten free prepared spells, and four of them are off-list.** *Heroism*, *Suggestion*, *Compulsion*, and *Rary's Telepathic Bond* are not on the Cleric spell list at all. The other six — *Command*, *Zone of Truth*, *Sending*, *Tongues*, *Divination*, *Geas* — are, and getting them free is arguably worth more, because it means **your own prepared list should be almost purely combat and healing.** Do not prepare *Sending*, *Zone of Truth*, or *Divination*. You already have them, permanently, for nothing.

**6. Hope and Havoc is a team feature, not a solo one.** It applies to "the next D20 Test," which the rules glossary defines as ability checks, attack rolls, *and saving throws*. That last one is where the value is — Havoc on a boss is a −3.5 to its next save. But applying it costs your Magic action, so you cannot Channel Divinity and cast a leveled spell on the same turn. The pattern that actually wins fights is:

  > **You** Havoc the target → **an ally** immediately lands the save-or-suck.

  Used at the top of a round with no follow-up, Havoc usually just gives one enemy Disadvantage on one attack roll, which is fine but not worth a Channel Divinity. Hope has the same shape in reverse: it is at its best fired immediately before a Rogue's turn (Advantage enables Sneak Attack), before the party's alpha strike, or before a telegraphed area save.

  Two more notes: it competes with Divine Spark and Turn Undead for **two** uses until level 6 (three thereafter, four at 19); and Charisma saves are the deciding factor for Havoc. Beasts, oozes, constructs, and most brutes have miserable Charisma. Fiends, dragons, celestials, and fey have excellent Charisma. Pick your Havoc target on that basis, and put Hope on your allies when the room is full of dragons.

**7. Blessed Strikes should be Potent Spellcasting for essentially every Word cleric.** The domain gives you nothing that improves a weapon attack — no Divine Strike analogue, no attack riders, no extra attacks. Every one of its features rewards casting and speaking. Divine Strike is only worth it if you have built a Strength 16+ Protector, and the two points of Wisdom that costs come straight out of the save DC the entire subclass runs on. All three builds below take Potent Spellcasting; two of them take Protector purely for the heavy armour.

**8. You are a Concentration caster in a domain built out of Concentration spells.** *Heroism*, *Suggestion*, *Compulsion*, *Silence*, *Spirit Guardians*, *Bless*, *Hold Person* — all Concentration, all mutually exclusive. Half of learning this subclass is learning which one you are running this fight. War Caster and Resilient (Constitution) are not luxuries.

**9. Command is the domain's best combat spell for the first six levels, and stays good forever.** Verbal component only, no Concentration, 60 feet, Wisdom save, and each slot level above 1st adds another target. "Halt" deletes a creature's turn. Because it is Verbal-only it works while you are grappled, restrained, or holding a shield in both senses, and it works through *Silence* and through Blessed Herald. Prepare nothing else at level 3 and you will still contribute.

### Three questions to settle with your DM before session one

- **Does Blessed Herald relocate only areas of effect, or all ranges?** "Point of origin" is a defined term in the rules glossary, and the definition is attached to *areas of effect* — Cone, Cube, Cylinder, Emanation, Line, Sphere. On the narrow reading, Blessed Herald lets you place the shape of any Verbal Cleric spell on your herald rather than yourself. That alone is extraordinary: *Spirit Guardians* is a 15-foot **Emanation**, and an Emanation "moves with the creature that is its origin" — so your barbarian becomes a walking damage aura while you stand two hundred feet away. On the broad reading, every range is measured from the herald, which makes *Cure Wounds* (Touch) and *Command* (60 ft) into effectively unlimited-range spells. Either way you must still satisfy the spell's own targeting text — "a creature you can see" is not waived by the feature — which is the natural brake on the broad reading. Ask before you build around it.
- **Can a creature Deafened by your own *Silence* be affected by your *Command*?** *Suggestion* explicitly requires the target to hear you, so that one is settled: it fails. *Command*'s 2024 text says only "You speak a one-word command to a creature you can see within range" and doesn't name hearing as a requirement — but no sound leaves or crosses the Sphere. This determines whether the Silence build has a control option inside its own bubble or only cantrips.
- **Does Hope apply to Initiative?** Initiative is a D20 Test. Using Channel Divinity before a fight starts — the classic "we are about to kick this door" moment — would give up to six allies Advantage on Initiative. Reasonable DMs differ on whether you can spend an action before initiative is rolled.

# CONCEPT I — THE ENVOY

## Covenant Vell, Legate of the Measured Light

**Tiefling (infernal legacy) · Acolyte · Cleric (Word Domain) · she/her · Lawful Neutral**

> *"I am not going to lie to you. That is the whole difficulty."*

### The pitch

A temple advocate with devil's blood and a god's licence, who has spent her career proving that a binding word can be holy rather than damning. She talks first, and when talking stops working she takes away her opponent's ability to decide anything for themselves. Built around Hope and Havoc as a fight-swinging action and around the domain's enchantment package — *Command*, *Suggestion*, *Compulsion*, *Geas* — as an actual win condition rather than flavour.

This is the Word Domain played as **the mouth**: the party's negotiator, its interrogator, and the reason the enemy commander spends the third round of every fight walking away from his own soldiers.

### Origin and lore

Covenant was born in the Daerrow, in the flood ward below the Sullow Quarter, to one of the tiefling families whose infernal line runs back to a contract-devil rather than a warlord — a distinction her neighbours did not make and her mother made constantly. Her legacy shows in the ordinary way: horns swept back close to the skull, gold eyes, and the faint smell of struck flint that follows her into rooms. Her family's whole inheritance is a word kept by something that should not have kept it.

She was taken into the Temple of Measured Light at nine, as a charity intake, which in the Thorngate District means a child who is fed and taught and never quite listed. She was very good at the work. Doctrine of the Measured Light is essentially a theology of accounting — what is said should match what is true, what is promised should match what is delivered, and the discrepancy between the two is the only sin the god recognises. For a Daerrow tiefling raised on a devil's kept bargain, this was not a difficult faith to accept.

She is now a legate: the Temple's external voice, sent to negotiate with the Guild of Chartered Commerce, the Harbour Authority, and the six or seven families whose wealth predates the Council. High Archivist Solen Drath uses her because she is unforgettable and therefore memorable in exactly the way an institution wants its threats to be. She is aware of this. She has made her peace with being the thing they send.

What she has not made peace with is the ledger. Twelve months ago she began reconciling the Temple's external undertakings against its internal accounts as a matter of routine, and the routine stopped working. There is a discrepancy — not enormous, entirely consistent, and running through the blessed vaults that the great families keep under religious exemption. By her own doctrine, a discrepancy between the stated and the actual is the definition of sin. By her own doctrine, she is obliged to name it. She has not yet named it, and every day she does not is a day she is failing the only test her god sets.

**What drives her to adventure:** she needs to be outside the Temple's walls, working with people who owe the institution nothing, long enough to establish what the discrepancy actually is. She has told the Archivist she is pursuing a charitable commission in the lower city. That was the first deliberate lie of her adult life, and she has not slept properly since.

*Design note on Intelligence 8.* This is normally a problem for a temple scholar and here it isn't, because **Thaumaturge adds your Wisdom modifier to Intelligence (Arcana or Religion) checks**. Plus *Word of Radiance* and *Spare the Dying* from Magic Initiate, and *Thaumaturgy* and *Fire Bolt* from her species — eight cantrips at level 1, which is absurd and is what happens when you stack a Thaumaturge, an Acolyte, and a Tiefling.

- **Her own prepared list at level 3 (6 spells):** Bless, Healing Word, Guiding Bolt, Shield of Faith, Spiritual Weapon, Hold Person. Note that none of these duplicate the domain — every prepared slot goes to combat or healing, exactly as the domain intends.

**Equipment.** Cleric option A (Chain Shirt, Shield, Mace, Holy Symbol, Priest's Pack, 7 GP) plus Acolyte option A (Calligrapher's Supplies, Book of prayers, Holy Symbol, 10 sheets of Parchment, Robe, 8 GP). She starts with everything she needs and 15 GP, which is correct — a legate is equipped by her institution and owns almost nothing.

#### How the domain plays for her

- **Hope and Havoc** is her second-best action and she uses it as a set-up, not a finisher. The pattern: Havoc the enemy commander, then the party's wizard drops Hypnotic Pattern or Hold Person into a target rolling its save at Disadvantage. From level 6 she has three uses and can hit three creatures at once.
- **Divine Orator** is why she works as a face on Charisma 13. She has Advantage on every spoken Persuasion check and Insight +5 rising to +10 by level 13 to know when someone is working her. She is not charming. She is *correct*, loudly, with a devil's face.
- **Word Domain Spells** are her win condition rather than her utility. *Command* from level 3 (Halt, on a Wisdom save, no Concentration, upcast to hit multiple targets). *Suggestion* to end a fight before it starts. *Compulsion* at 7 — a Wisdom save against every creature of her choice in 30 feet, Charmed for a minute. *Geas* at 9 for the political work.
- **Blessed Herald** she uses socially more than tactically: her herald each morning is whichever party member is going into the room she cannot enter, and she can hear and speak to them across the whole city.
- **Word Perfect** at 17 means every leveled Verbal spell she casts carries a free Hope or Havoc. Since she is casting an enchantment nearly every turn by then, this is close to unconditional.

#### Signature turn (level 7)

WIS 19, PB +3, spell save DC 15. **Round 1:** *Bless* on the front line, cast off the free Magic Initiate use, spending no slot. **Round 2:** Channel Divinity — Hope on the rogue and the barbarian, Havoc on the captain. The rogue's next attack has Advantage, which is Sneak Attack; the barbarian's has Advantage; the captain's next D20 Test is at Disadvantage. **Round 3:** *Compulsion*, and the captain — still Disadvantaged if he hasn't rolled since — plus everyone within 30 feet of him makes a Wisdom save or spends the next minute being walked out of the fight.

#### What this build gives up

Almost no damage of her own — Sacred Flame and Toll the Dead, plus a Spiritual Weapon, and that is the list until Potent Spellcasting at 7. Twenty-four hit points at level 3 in medium armour. Strength 8 and Intelligence 8 mean two of the six saving throws are actively bad, and *Command*, *Suggestion*, and *Compulsion* are all Wisdom saves, so a party facing Wisdom-proficient enemies watches her whole kit bounce. She needs a party that can follow up on her set-ups; in a group of independent operators she is a very expensive Bless.

#### Roleplay

- **Personality:** Formal, careful, and physically still. Speaks in complete sentences and finishes them even when interrupted. Has a lawyer's habit of restating your position more accurately than you did before dismantling it.
- **Ideal:** *The discrepancy is the sin.* Not the lie, not the theft — the gap between what is said and what is so.
- **Bond:** The Temple of Measured Light, which raised her, and which she is currently investigating. Both of those are love.
- **Flaw:** She has now told one lie, and it has cost her the ability to believe her own certainty. She hesitates at exactly the wrong moments.
- **Trinket suggestion:** A ledger page in her mother's hand, recording a debt paid in full by someone who had no reason to pay it.

# CONCEPT II — THE GAG

## Yoth-Kanner, called Quiet Kanner

**Orc · Farmer · Cleric (Word Domain) · he/him · Lawful Neutral**

> *"You can say it to me. You just can't say it to anyone else."*

### The pitch

A heavy-armoured orc who walks into the middle of the enemy's formation, drops a twenty-foot sphere of absolute silence over everyone including himself, and then keeps casting — because he is the only creature in the world that Silence does not stop. Everything else in the build exists to let him stand there: AC 18, Constitution 16, the Tough feat, and a feat plan that reads like a list of ways to not lose Concentration.

This is the Word Domain played as **the gag**: a subclass feature most people read as flavour text, turned into a battlefield role.

### Origin and lore

Kanner is from Ashgrove, the borderland settlement at the head of the eastern valley, from one of the Orcish families that came west a generation ago and stayed because the valley grew on them. He grew up doing what everyone in Ashgrove does — animals, timber, the two harvests — and he was, by every account including his own, unremarkable at it. He was slow, careful, physically enormous, and inclined to stand still and listen for a long time before saying anything. In a valley that measures people by whether they can do useful work, this was tolerated rather than admired.

What changed him was a bad winter and a man who talked. A preacher came up the track from the lowlands with a fluent, comfortable, entirely false account of why Ashgrove's grain had failed and who was owed for it, and by the time anyone had thought it through properly the community had signed away two seasons. Nobody in Ashgrove was stupid. They had simply never been argued at by a professional, and had no answer to it. Kanner was nineteen and watched the whole thing happen from the back of the room, and what he took from it was not a grievance. It was a technical observation: **the man's only weapon was that he was allowed to keep talking.**

He walked to Corraith — three hundred miles, most of a year — and presented himself at the Temple of Measured Light, whose doctrine holds that the gap between what is said and what is true is the only sin. They trained him for six years. He is not a legate; he was never going to be. He is what the Temple calls a proctor and what everyone else calls an inquisitor: the one who goes where the fluent people are working and makes the room quiet enough to think in.

The Temple sent him home. The Thornvael has no cathedrals and no appetite for institutions — divine practice there is roadside shrines and a chapel at Kettleford that serves all comers — so an inquisitor with a badge from a city three hundred miles away has exactly no authority, and he knows it. He works on sufferance, mostly on Constable Aldra Venn's goodwill, mostly by being the largest and least talkative person in whatever room needs one.

**What drives him to adventure:** the depositions coming out of the western farms describe things that don't happen, and everyone he asks tells him the truth as they understand it. That is the part that frightens him. He has spent six years learning to find the person who is lying, and there isn't one. Something is putting false accounts into true mouths, and his entire training assumes that isn't possible.

Every pick after level 4 serves one sentence: *stand in the middle of the room and do not stop concentrating.*

#### How the domain plays for him

- **Divine Orator** is the build. *Silence* is a 20-foot-radius Sphere in which "casting a spell that includes a Verbal component is impossible" — and he is exempt. He drops it on the enemy line and becomes the only source of magic inside a 40-foot-wide bubble. Enemy casters are reduced to whatever they can do with Somatic components alone, which for most of them is nothing.
- **The cost is Concentration, and it is a real cost.** *Silence* and *Spirit Guardians* both require it, so he chooses at the start of every fight which kind of wall he is: the one that does 3d8 radiant damage to everything near him, or the one that switches off the enemy's spellcasting entirely. That choice is the most interesting recurring decision in the build.
- **Level 6 resolves the dilemma, if your DM allows the narrow reading of Blessed Herald.** *Spirit Guardians* is an Emanation, and an Emanation moves with the creature that is its origin. Herald the party's barbarian in the morning, cast *Spirit Guardians* centred on them, and then hold *Silence* yourself — the party fields both auras at once, from one cleric. This is the single strongest interaction in the subclass and the reason to settle the ruling in session zero.
- **Hope and Havoc** he uses almost entirely as Havoc, on whatever he is about to hit with a save. From level 6, three targets.
- **Zone of Truth**, free and permanent from level 3, is his profession. Note the spell tells *you* whether each creature succeeded or failed the save, which means it is an information tool even when it fails.
- **Word Perfect** at 17 gives him a free Hope or Havoc on every leveled Verbal spell — including the *Silence* he casts every fight.

#### Signature turn (level 7)

WIS 19, PB +3, DC 15, AC 18, 73 HP. **Bonus Action:** Adrenaline Rush — Dash, plus 3 temporary hit points, closing 60 feet to stand between the enemy's two spellcasters. **Action:** *Silence*, centred on himself. The sphere covers both casters, four soldiers, and him. Nobody in it can speak, hear, or cast a Verbal spell — except him. **Round 2 onward:** *Toll the Dead* at 1d12+4, once per turn, at whichever caster looks most frantic, while the party kills the soldiers who can no longer be buffed, healed, or teleported out.

He does perhaps eleven damage a turn. He also removes both enemy casters from the encounter and cannot be talked around, shouted past, or counterspelled.

#### What this build gives up

Real damage — his contribution is measured in what the enemy *doesn't* get to do. Disadvantage on Stealth in chain mail, permanently, which routes the party's quiet plans around him. Initiative +0 until something fixes it, meaning he often spends round one walking. Intelligence 8 and Charisma 10 with only Advantage-on-Persuasion to trade on. And a genuine dead zone at levels 3–4, where he has *Silence* but no *Spirit Guardians*, no Potent Spellcasting, and a warhammer he can barely swing.

#### Roleplay

- **Personality:** Enormous, slow, and unnervingly patient. Listens for much longer than is comfortable and then asks one question. Has never in his life raised his voice, and does not need to.
- **Ideal:** *Everyone gets to think.* Fluency is not truth. The person who talks fastest should not therefore win.
- **Bond:** Ashgrove, which lost two seasons to a man with a good voice, and which does not know he came back for it.
- **Flaw:** He is certain that if he can just make a room quiet enough, the truth will surface. It very often doesn't, and he does not have a second plan.
- **Trinket suggestion:** The signed agreement from that winter, folded into eighths, carried since he was nineteen.

# CONCEPT III — THE SIGNAL

## Wick Ambry, Chaplain of the Coast Road

**Gnome (rock lineage) · Missionary · Cleric (Word Domain) · they/them · Neutral Good**

> *"I'll be four hundred yards behind you. You won't be alone."*

### The pitch

A three-and-a-half-foot chapel priest who has turned Blessed Herald into a job. Every morning they lay a hand on whoever is going in first, and for the rest of the day that person can hear them, anywhere on the plane — and every area spell they cast lands where that person is standing. They are the party's healer, its long-range artillery, and its entire communications network, from a position where nothing can reach them.

This is the Word Domain played as **the relay**: the least glamorous of the three and the one that most changes how a party can operate.

### Origin and lore

The Fenway coast has no cathedral and no bishop. What it has is a chain of port chapels — Kelstead, Thornwater, Stoneveil Reach, the modest ecumenical one at Brindlemark — that serve whoever walks in, keep whatever rites the community has always kept, and, crucially, hold the correspondence. On a coast where half the population is at sea or on the road, the chapel is where a message waits for you. It is a religious institution mostly by convention and a postal service mostly by necessity, and Wick Ambry has spent twenty-two years being the person who carries it between them.

They are a rock gnome, born above a chandler's shop in Kelstead Port to a family of instrument-makers, and they came to the work sideways — first as a boy who fixed the chapel's bell mechanism, then as the one who kept its register, then, at some point nobody can date precisely, as the one people came to. There was no ordination. There was a morning when old Mother Reeve did not get up and the queue outside the chapel door formed anyway, and Wick opened it.

The god, if it is a god, has never introduced itself. What Wick has is a persistent conviction that things said aloud are held somewhere, and a set of abilities that behave as though that is true. They are not troubled by the theology. Fenway faith is practical: you keep the shrine, you feed who comes, you carry the letter, and whatever is on the other end can sort out its own paperwork.

Their reputation on the coast road is out of all proportion to their size. Wick has walked the Fenway from Stoneveil to Ashford more times than they can count, cooking for whoever is on the road, setting bones at Brindlemark, and carrying a satchel of other people's words. They know every innkeeper, every drover, and the specific look a harbour clerk gets when a manifest is wrong. They have never been robbed, which they attribute to grace and everyone else attributes to the fact that robbing Wick Ambry would make you the most unpopular person on the coast.

**What drives them to adventure:** three letters, posted from three different chapels over eight months, addressed to people who do not exist. Wick has held all three, and the handwriting is the same, and the ink is the same, and the reason they know that is that they have been keeping the coast's correspondence for twenty-two years and nobody else in the world has read enough of it to notice. Somebody is using the chapel network to move something, and they are using it because they assume no one is reading.

**Species traits.** *Gnomish Cunning* — Advantage on Intelligence, Wisdom, and Charisma saving throws — is one of the strongest defensive traits in the game for a back-line caster, and it covers the three saves that end a Concentration spell by turning the caster off entirely. *Rock Gnome* gives *Mending* and *Prestidigitation* free, plus the clockwork device trait, which for a chapel-keeper who came up fixing bell mechanisms is not a gimmick but a character. *Darkvision* 60 feet.

**Background.** Missionary grants the **Healer** origin feat, proficiency in **Persuasion** and **Survival**, and Cook's Utensils. Ability increases: **+2 WIS, +1 CHA**.

*Missionary is the best background in the book for this concept and it is homebrew, which is worth saying.* Persuasion is the proficiency Divine Orator needs; Survival is the road; Cook's Utensils is literally the character's ministry ("a full belly and a healed hand win more converts than any sermon"); and the Healer feat gives slot-free healing that scales with Proficiency Bonus — Battle Medic lets an ally spend a Hit Die and adds your PB to the roll, as a Utilize action, without touching a spell slot.

#### How the domain plays for them

- **Blessed Herald is the character.** Every morning Wick touches whoever is going in first — the scout, the front-liner, whoever the day's plan puts furthest forward. For the rest of the day the two of them can speak aloud across any distance on the same plane. This is a *scouting revolution* on its own, before any spell is cast: the rogue can walk into the enemy camp and narrate.
- **Then the spells.** On the narrow, defined-term reading, every area of effect Wick casts with a Verbal component originates on the herald: *Spirit Guardians* as a moving 15-foot Emanation around the front-liner; *Silence* dropped precisely on the enemy caster from four hundred feet; *Flame Strike*'s Cylinder placed by someone who is actually looking at the target. On the broad reading, *Cure Wounds* and *Command* also reach from wherever the herald is standing. **Settle this before you build.** Even the narrow reading justifies the whole concept.
- **The limit is Channel Divinity, and it hurts.** Hope and Havoc affects "creatures you can see within 60 feet of *yourself*" — Blessed Herald moves spell origins, not features. So Wick's one battlefield-wide swing requires them to come within 60 feet and have line of sight, which is closer than they want to be. Planning around that tension — artillery at 200 feet, Channel Divinity at 60 — is the tactical texture of the build.
- **Divine Orator** they use less than the Envoy does, and it saves their life more often: it means no gag, no *Silence*, and no held mouth stops them casting. A support caster who cannot be shut up is a support caster who cannot be neutralised.
- **Hope** is their preferred half of the Channel Divinity, because they are running a party rather than fighting one: up to six allies with Advantage on their next D20 Test, fired the round before the plan happens.
- **Word Perfect** at 17 finally frees them from the Channel Divinity economy: every leveled Verbal spell — which is every spell they cast — carries a free Hope or Havoc for one creature within 60 feet.

#### Signature turn (level 10)

WIS 20, PB +4, DC 17. Wick is 180 feet up the road behind a stone wall, with the party's fighter as their herald. **Bonus Action:** *Healing Word* on the ranger — 60 feet from the herald, not from Wick. **Action:** *Spirit Guardians* at 5th level, centred on the fighter, 5d8 radiant to everything in a 15-foot Emanation that walks wherever the fighter walks, Wisdom save, speed halved. Wick has not been seen by anything in the encounter and is not in it. **Later rounds:** *Toll the Dead* at 1d12+5 through the herald's position on whatever is still standing, and Battle Medic on the first person who reaches them.

#### What this build gives up

They are Small, slow, and have twenty-four hit points; anything that reaches them kills them. Charisma 11 means the party face job belongs to someone else. The whole build is a bet on Blessed Herald, which is one feature, arrives at level 6, and depends on a ruling — before that, Wick is a competent, unremarkable support cleric. And the herald is a single point of failure: if that person goes down or leaves the plane, half of Wick's action economy goes with them, and the feature does not come back until a Long Rest.

#### Roleplay

- **Personality:** Cheerful, practical, and entirely unimpressed by rank. Feeds people before asking them anything. Keeps a register and writes in it constantly.
- **Ideal:** *Nothing said should be lost.* The word gets where it was going. That is the whole of the ministry.
- **Bond:** The chapel register at Kelstead Port, twenty-two years of it, and the three letters addressed to nobody.
- **Flaw:** Cannot refuse a request for help, cannot triage, and will spend the party's last resource on a stranger without asking anyone.
- **Trinket suggestion:** A small brass clockwork bell that rings once, faintly, at the hour Mother Reeve died, and has done every day since.

## Part 4: Choosing Between Them

**Play the Envoy** if you want the subclass's social features to be the reason you picked it, you want to be the party face without spending a point on Charisma, and you enjoy characters whose combat contribution is a *setup* the rest of the table cashes in. She needs a party that follows up; in return she decides who gets a turn.

**Play the Gag** if you want one of the domain's throwaway clauses turned into a battlefield role, you like being the reason the enemy's plan doesn't work, and you're comfortable with a build that trades damage for denial and asks you to stand somewhere frightening while doing it. He is the most durable of the three and the least impressive on a damage log.

**Play the Signal** if you want to change what your *party* can do rather than what you can do — permanent long-range communication, area spells that land where somebody else is standing, and a healer who is never in the encounter. The lowest-risk position, the highest dependence on a single feature, and the most fun to plan around.

Two of them serve the same institution and would not agree about anything: Covenant thinks the Measured Light's job is to hold the powerful to their word, and Kanner thinks its job is to stop the powerful from talking. The third has never met a bishop and would feed them both.

All three want the same three things settled at session zero: **how far Blessed Herald reaches**, **whether your *Command* survives your own *Silence***, and **whether Hope can be spent on Initiative**. Everything else is preference.
